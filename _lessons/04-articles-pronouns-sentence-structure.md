This lesson covers two of the trickiest grammar points for Persian speakers: Dutch's two-gender article system, and its strict verb-second word order.

<span dir="rtl" lang="fa">این درس دو نکته گرامری دشوار برای فارسی‌زبانان را پوشش می‌دهد: سیستم دو جنسیتی حروف تعریف و ترتیب سخت‌گیرانه فعل-دوم.</span>

## De-words and het-words

Persian has no articles like "the" at all — Dutch has two, and every noun is fixed to one:

| Article | Used for | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **de** | ~75% of nouns (originally masculine + feminine) | de man, de vrouw, de tafel |
| **het** | ~25% of nouns (originally neuter), all diminutives (-je) | het huis, het boek, het meisje |
| **een** | "a/an" — same word for both types | een man, een huis |

> ⚠️ There's no reliable rule for de vs het — you must learn the article **with** the word, like learning "de tafel" as one unit, not "tafel" alone. In plural, **all** nouns use **de**: de huizen, de boeken.

## Sentence structure: the V2 rule

Persian word order is fairly flexible (subject-object-verb, often with the verb last). **Dutch is stricter**: in a main clause, the conjugated verb must be the **second element** — no matter what comes first.

| Dutch | Pronunciation | English | Persian |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ik drink koffie. ✅ | ik drink KOF-fee | I drink coffee — subject first, verb second. | <span dir="rtl" lang="fa">من قهوه می‌نوشم.</span> |
| Vandaag drink ik koffie. ✅ | van-DAAKH drink ik KOF-fee | Today I drink coffee — time word first, so the verb stays 2nd and **ik** moves after it. | <span dir="rtl" lang="fa">امروز من قهوه می‌نوشم.</span> |
| ~~Vandaag ik drink koffie~~ ❌ | van-DAAKH ik drink KOF-fee | wrong, verb must stay in position 2. | — |

<span dir="rtl" lang="fa">این «قانون V2» یعنی فعل صرف‌شده همیشه دومین عنصر جمله است، حتی اگر جمله با چیزی غیر از فاعل شروع شود — فاعل و فعل جابه‌جا می‌شوند.</span>

## Summary

- Every noun pairs with **de** or **het** — memorize them together.
- **een** = a/an for both types; plural nouns always take **de**.
- Dutch main clauses obey **V2**: conjugated verb = 2nd position, always — subject and verb invert if something else leads the sentence.
