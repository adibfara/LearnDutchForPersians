Persian verb conjugation works very differently (root + person-endings on a different system) — Dutch present tense is actually simpler once you learn the pattern.

## The regular pattern

1. Take the infinitive (dictionary form), e.g. **werken** (to work)
2. Remove **-en** to get the stem (stam): **werk**
3. Conjugate:

| Pronoun | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| ik | stem | ik werk |
| jij / u | stem + t | jij werkt / u werkt |
| hij / zij / het | stem + t | hij werkt |
| wij | infinitive | wij werken |
| jullie | infinitive | jullie werken |
| zij (they) | infinitive | zij werken |

> 💡 Rule of thumb: **"ik" = bare stem, "jij/hij/zij" = stem + t, everyone plural = full infinitive."**

## Spelling note

Some stems change spelling to keep the vowel sound correct (open/closed syllables) — e.g. **wonen** (to live) → stem **woon** (double vowel kept), not "won". This is a spelling rule, not a new grammar rule — you'll absorb it with practice.

## Two essential irregular verbs

### zijn (to be)

| Pronoun | Form |
|---|---|
| ik | ben |
| jij / u | bent |
| hij/zij/het | is |
| wij/jullie/zij | zijn |

### hebben (to have)

| Pronoun | Form |
|---|---|
| ik | heb |
| jij / u | hebt / heeft |
| hij/zij/het | heeft |
| wij/jullie/zij | hebben |

> ⚠️ Dutch uses **hebben** (to have) for age and some feelings differently than you might expect from Persian — actually age uses **zijn**: "Ik ben 20 jaar" (I am 20 years), not "I have 20 years" as in Persian «۲۰ سال دارم» or French. But hunger/thirst use **hebben**: "Ik heb honger" (I have hunger = I'm hungry) — closer to the Persian pattern.

## Example sentences

| Dutch | English |
|---|---|
| Ik werk in Utrecht. | I work in Utrecht. |
| Jij leert Nederlands. | You are learning Dutch. |
| Zij woont in Rotterdam. | She lives in Rotterdam. |
| Wij spreken Perzisch en Nederlands. | We speak Persian and Dutch. |
| Ik ben moe. | I am tired. |
| Hij heeft een fiets. | He has a bicycle. |

## Summary

- Regular verbs: ik = stem, jij/hij/zij = stem+t, plural = infinitive.
- **zijn** (to be) and **hebben** (to have) are irregular and used constantly — memorize both fully.
- Age uses zijn ("Ik ben 20 jaar"); hunger/thirst use hebben ("Ik heb honger").
